The **No Surprises Act** took effect on January 1, 2022. It protects patients from many unexpected out-of-network bills and created new requirements for providers and facilities. For practices that see patients out of network — or that serve uninsured and self-pay patients — understanding the law is essential.

This article summarizes the main provisions. It is not legal advice, and you should review current federal guidance and any state laws that apply to your practice.

## Which services are protected?

The law generally protects patients with private health coverage from **balance billing** — being billed for the difference between the provider’s charge and the plan’s payment — in these situations:

1. **Emergency services**, whether provided at an in-network or out-of-network facility, including certain post-stabilization services
2. **Non-emergency services provided by out-of-network providers at in-network facilities**, such as hospitals and ambulatory surgical centers — for example, anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, and assistant surgeons
3. **Air ambulance services** from out-of-network providers

In these situations, the patient pays only their **in-network cost sharing** (copay, coinsurance, and deductible), and the out-of-network provider cannot bill the patient for more.

## Notice and consent

For certain non-emergency services at in-network facilities, out-of-network providers may ask patients to waive their protections by signing a **notice and consent** form, provided specific timing and content requirements are met. However, this option is **not available** for:

- Emergency services
- Ancillary services such as anesthesiology, pathology, radiology, neonatology, and services by assistant surgeons, hospitalists, and intensivists
- Situations where no in-network provider is available at the facility

## Good faith estimates for uninsured and self-pay patients

Providers and facilities must give **uninsured patients and patients who choose not to use their insurance** a **good faith estimate** of expected charges for scheduled services, and upon request.

Key points:

- The estimate must be provided within set timeframes depending on how far in advance the service is scheduled
- It should include expected charges for the primary service and, where applicable, items and services from co-providers
- If the final bill is **substantially higher** than the estimate — currently defined as **$400 or more** above the estimate for a provider — the patient may use a federal **patient-provider dispute resolution** process

Practices should have a standard process and template for creating and delivering good faith estimates, and should keep copies on file.

## How out-of-network payment is determined

For services covered by the law, the health plan makes an initial payment or denial to the out-of-network provider. If the provider disagrees with the amount:

1. **Open negotiation:** Either party can start a 30-business-day negotiation period
2. **Independent dispute resolution (IDR):** If negotiation fails, either party can start the federal IDR process, in which a certified entity selects either the provider’s or the payer’s offer

The IDR process has strict deadlines, eligibility rules, and administrative fees. Disputes can sometimes be **batched** when they involve similar services. Federal rules and operational details for IDR have been revised several times since 2022, so check current guidance before starting a dispute.

## State laws

Many states had surprise billing laws before the federal act. In some situations — particularly for state-regulated insurance plans — a state law may determine payment amounts instead of the federal process. Check how your state’s law interacts with the federal rules.

## What practices should do

- **Identify** which of your services and locations are affected — especially out-of-network work at in-network facilities
- **Train front-desk staff** to identify uninsured and self-pay patients and deliver good faith estimates
- **Update billing workflows** so protected claims are not balance billed to patients
- **Post required disclosures** about patient protections on your website and in your office, where applicable
- **Track open negotiation and IDR deadlines** for out-of-network claims
- **Keep documentation** of estimates, consents, and payer communications

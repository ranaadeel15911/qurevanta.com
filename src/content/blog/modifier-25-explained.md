Modifier 25 is one of the most valuable — and most scrutinized — modifiers in medical billing. Used correctly, it ensures you are paid for real work. Used incorrectly, it can lead to denials, recoupments, and audits.

## What modifier 25 means

Modifier 25 is appended to an **evaluation and management (E/M) code** to indicate a *significant, separately identifiable E/M service by the same physician or other qualified health care professional on the same day of a procedure or other service*.

Every procedure already includes a certain amount of evaluation. For minor procedures (those with a 0- or 10-day global period), the decision to perform the procedure and the pre-procedure assessment are part of the procedure payment. Modifier 25 tells the payer that you did **more** than that — you evaluated and managed a problem that goes beyond the usual pre-procedure work.

## When modifier 25 is appropriate

Common, legitimate scenarios include:

- **A procedure plus a separate problem.** A patient comes in for a scheduled knee injection and also asks about worsening high blood pressure, which the physician evaluates and treats with a medication change.
- **An evaluation that leads to a minor procedure.** A new patient with several skin concerns is evaluated, and the physician decides to biopsy one lesion. The broader evaluation may be separately billable when it goes beyond the decision to biopsy, depending on documentation and payer policy.
- **A preventive visit plus a problem visit.** During an annual physical, a patient raises a new problem that requires significant additional work, such as evaluation of new chest discomfort. The problem-oriented visit can be billed with modifier 25 alongside the preventive service.
- **Immunizations or tests with a separate E/M.** When vaccines or diagnostic tests are performed on the same day as a sick visit, the E/M may require modifier 25 depending on payer rules.

## When modifier 25 is not appropriate

- When the only evaluation performed is the **routine pre-procedure assessment** — for example, examining the knee before the scheduled injection
- When the visit is for **post-operative follow-up** within a global period (use modifier 24 only for unrelated E/M services)
- On **procedure codes** — modifier 25 belongs on the E/M code only
- When the decision for **major surgery** (90-day global) was made — that situation uses **modifier 57** instead

## Does it need a different diagnosis?

No. A separate diagnosis is **not required** for modifier 25. What matters is that the E/M service is significant and separately identifiable. That said, a different diagnosis often makes the distinction clearer, and some payers look for it in their editing.

## How to document modifier 25 visits

A note that supports modifier 25 should make the separate service obvious to a reviewer who has never met the patient:

1. **Separate the content.** Ideally, document the procedure in a distinct procedure note, and the E/M service in its own history, assessment, and plan.
2. **State the additional problem clearly.** "Patient also reports three weeks of increasing shortness of breath" is better than a list of complaints.
3. **Show medical decision-making.** Describe tests reviewed or ordered, management decisions, and risk.
4. **Choose the E/M level based only on the separate service.** Do not count the work of the procedure toward the E/M level.

## Payment and audit considerations

Modifier 25 has been the focus of reviews by the HHS Office of Inspector General and of payer policies over many years, because it is easy to overuse. Some commercial payers have introduced policies that reduce payment for E/M services billed with modifier 25 or require records for review.

Signs that your modifier 25 use may need attention:

- Nearly every procedure visit is billed with an E/M and modifier 25
- One provider uses it far more often than peers in the same specialty
- Notes for the E/M service and the procedure are largely identical
- Denials or record requests from payers are increasing

## Modifier 25 and the G2211 add-on

For Medicare, the visit complexity add-on code **G2211** is generally not payable when the associated office visit is billed with modifier 25 (with limited exceptions such as certain preventive services and vaccine administration). Check current CMS guidance and commercial payer policies.

CPT code 99214 is one of the most frequently billed codes in American outpatient medicine. It describes an office or other outpatient visit for an **established patient** that requires a **moderate level of medical decision-making (MDM)**, or at least **30 minutes of total practitioner time** on the date of the encounter.

Because it is billed so often, and because it pays meaningfully more than 99213, 99214 also attracts attention from payers and auditors. Understanding exactly what supports it protects both your revenue and your practice.

## How 99214 is selected today

Since January 2021, office visit codes 99202–99215 are selected based on **either** medical decision-making **or** total time — whichever better reflects the visit. History and physical examination no longer count toward the level, although a medically appropriate history and exam should still be documented.

### Option 1: Moderate medical decision-making

MDM has three elements. To bill 99214, **two of the three** must meet or exceed the moderate level.

**1. Number and complexity of problems addressed.** Moderate examples include:

- One or more chronic illnesses with exacerbation, progression, or side effects of treatment
- Two or more stable chronic illnesses
- An undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis
- An acute illness with systemic symptoms
- An acute complicated injury

**2. Amount and complexity of data reviewed and analyzed.** Moderate requires meeting at least one of three categories:

- Any combination of three items from: reviewing prior external notes from each unique source, reviewing the result of each unique test, ordering each unique test, or assessment requiring an independent historian
- Independent interpretation of a test performed by another physician or qualified health professional (not separately reported)
- Discussion of management or test interpretation with an external physician or other appropriate source

**3. Risk of complications from patient management.** Moderate examples include:

- Prescription drug management
- A decision regarding minor surgery with identified patient or procedure risk factors
- A decision regarding elective major surgery without identified risk factors
- Diagnosis or treatment significantly limited by social determinants of health

### Option 2: Total time

Under current CPT rules, 99214 may be reported when the practitioner spends **at least 30 minutes** of total time on the date of the encounter. Total time includes both face-to-face and non-face-to-face work performed by the physician or other qualified health professional that day, such as:

- Preparing to see the patient and reviewing tests
- Obtaining or reviewing a separately obtained history
- Performing the examination and counseling the patient
- Ordering medications, tests, or procedures
- Documenting in the medical record
- Communicating with other professionals (when not separately reported)

Time spent by clinical staff, and time spent on separately billed services, does not count.

## A practical example

A patient with type 2 diabetes and hypertension returns for a follow-up. The patient’s A1c has risen since the last visit. The physician reviews the new lab results, adjusts the patient’s oral medication, and orders a repeat A1c and a lipid panel.

- **Problems:** One chronic illness with progression (diabetes) plus a stable chronic illness — at least moderate.
- **Data:** Review of one test result and ordering of two unique tests — three items, which meets moderate.
- **Risk:** Prescription drug management — moderate.

All three elements meet moderate, so 99214 is supported by MDM even if the visit took less than 30 minutes.

## 99213 versus 99214

The difference between 99213 and 99214 is usually the clearest example of why documentation matters. A visit for a single stable chronic illness with a medication refill is generally **low** complexity, supporting 99213. Once the physician is managing two stable chronic conditions, addressing a worsening condition, or making prescription changes, the visit often reaches **moderate** complexity.

Studies and payer data have long suggested that many practices undercode out of caution. Undercoding is not a compliance safe harbor — it simply leaves earned revenue uncollected. The goal is accurate coding, not conservative or aggressive coding.

## Documentation tips

1. **Name every problem addressed** and state its status — stable, worsening, or not at goal. "Diabetes" alone does not show progression; "diabetes, not at goal, A1c increased from 7.4 to 8.3" does.
2. **Show the data work.** Note which results were reviewed, which tests were ordered, and whose records were reviewed.
3. **Describe the management decision.** "Increase metformin to 1000 mg twice daily" demonstrates prescription drug management.
4. **When billing on time, document the total time** and, ideally, the activities performed. "Total time on date of service: 34 minutes, including record review, exam, counseling, and documentation."
5. **Do not rely on templates alone.** Auto-populated text that does not reflect the actual visit can undermine the entire note in an audit.

## Common mistakes that lead to denials or audit findings

- **Counting problems that were not addressed.** A condition listed in the history but not evaluated or managed does not count toward MDM.
- **Billing time without documenting it.** If time is the basis for the code, the total time must appear in the note.
- **Counting staff time.** Only the billing practitioner’s time counts toward total time.
- **Using 99214 for every visit.** A flat distribution of E/M levels is a common audit trigger. Your level distribution should reflect your actual patient mix.
- **Ignoring modifier 25 rules.** When a procedure is performed on the same day, the E/M service must be significant and separately identifiable to be billed with [modifier 25](/resources/modifier-25-explained).

## The G2211 add-on code

Since 2024, Medicare has paid an add-on code, **G2211**, for visit complexity associated with ongoing, longitudinal care of a patient’s single serious or complex condition. It can be reported with office visit codes, including 99214, when the practitioner is the continuing focal point for the patient’s care. It generally should not be billed when the E/M visit is reported with modifier 25. Check each payer’s policy, as commercial plans vary.

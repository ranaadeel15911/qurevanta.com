A **clean claim** is a claim that is accepted and processed by the payer on the first submission, without needing corrections, additional information, or resubmission. Your **clean claim rate** is the percentage of claims that meet that standard.

It is one of the most useful indicators of how well your revenue cycle is working, because nearly every upstream problem — registration errors, missing authorizations, coding mistakes — shows up as a claim that is not clean.

## How to calculate it

The basic formula is:

**Clean claim rate = (claims accepted on first submission ÷ total claims submitted) × 100**

For example, if you submitted 2,000 claims last month and 1,900 passed both the clearinghouse and the payer’s initial edits without rework, your clean claim rate is 95%.

Be consistent about what you count. Some organizations measure only clearinghouse acceptance; a more meaningful measure includes payer rejections and denials that required rework.

## What is a good clean claim rate?

A widely cited benchmark for a well-run billing operation is **95% or higher**, and many high-performing organizations aim for 98%. If your rate is below 90%, a significant share of staff time is likely going to rework that could be avoided.

## Why it matters

Each claim that is not clean:

- **Delays payment** by days or weeks
- **Costs staff time** to investigate, correct, and resubmit
- **Increases the risk** of missing timely filing deadlines
- Often turns into a **denial**, which is even more expensive to resolve

Improving your clean claim rate is one of the most direct ways to reduce days in A/R and the cost to collect.

## Rejections versus denials

These terms are often used interchangeably, but they are different:

- A **rejection** happens before the claim is accepted into the payer’s system — usually because of formatting errors or invalid data. Rejected claims were never processed and must be corrected and resubmitted.
- A **denial** happens after the payer processes the claim and decides not to pay all or part of it. Denials must be corrected or appealed.

Both reduce your clean claim rate, and both need to be tracked.

## Eight ways to improve your clean claim rate

### 1. Get registration right

Most claim errors begin at the front desk: misspelled names, wrong dates of birth, incorrect subscriber IDs, or missing secondary insurance. Scan insurance cards at every visit and use a standard registration checklist.

### 2. Verify eligibility before every visit

Coverage changes frequently. Batch [eligibility verification](/resources/insurance-eligibility-verification) the day before appointments catches inactive coverage and plan changes while there is still time to act.

### 3. Secure authorizations up front

Build authorization checks into scheduling for services that commonly require them, such as imaging, procedures, and specialty drugs. See our [prior authorization workflow guide](/resources/prior-authorization-workflow).

### 4. Use a strong claim scrubber

Claim scrubbing software checks each claim against NCCI edits, payer-specific rules, and format requirements before submission. Review and update scrubber rules whenever you see a new denial pattern.

### 5. Improve coding accuracy

Make sure diagnosis codes are specific and support the procedures billed, and that modifiers are applied correctly. Periodic [coding audits](/services/coding-audit) identify recurring problems.

### 6. Keep provider and payer data current

Incorrect NPIs, taxonomy codes, or practice addresses cause rejections. Update your practice management system whenever credentialing information changes.

### 7. Submit quickly and work rejections daily

Claims should go out within a few days of service, and clearinghouse rejections should be corrected the same or next business day. A rejected claim sitting in a queue is invisible revenue.

### 8. Close the loop with feedback

Share rejection and denial trends with the people who can fix them — front desk staff, providers, and coders. Improvement happens when the root cause is addressed, not when the same claim is fixed over and over.

## Measuring progress

Track your clean claim rate monthly alongside related metrics such as denial rate, first-pass resolution rate, and days in A/R. Our guide to [revenue cycle KPIs](/resources/revenue-cycle-kpis) explains how these measures fit together.

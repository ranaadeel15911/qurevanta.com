Eligibility problems are among the most common reasons medical claims are denied — and among the easiest to prevent. A patient’s coverage can end, change, or move to a new plan without the practice knowing. Verifying insurance before every visit catches these problems before the service is provided, when they are still easy to fix.

## Why verifying once is not enough

Many practices verify coverage when a patient first registers and then assume it stays the same. In reality, coverage changes for many reasons:

- A new job or loss of employment
- Annual plan changes during open enrollment
- Medicaid redeterminations and changes between managed care plans
- Moving to a Medicare Advantage plan
- A spouse’s or parent’s coverage changing

A patient who was covered last month may not be covered today. That is why the best practice is to verify **before every visit**.

## What to verify

A complete verification goes beyond confirming that the policy is active. For each visit, check:

1. **Active coverage** on the date of service
2. **Plan type** — HMO, PPO, EPO, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid managed care — and the correct payer ID for claims
3. **Network status** — whether your providers are in-network for this specific plan
4. **Copay, coinsurance, and deductible** — including how much of the deductible has already been met
5. **Referral requirements** — especially for HMO and many Medicaid plans
6. **Prior authorization requirements** for the planned services
7. **Coverage for specific services** — for example, preventive care, behavioral health, or telehealth
8. **Coordination of benefits** — whether the patient has other coverage, and which plan is primary

## When to verify

- **At scheduling:** Collect insurance details and run an initial check, especially for new patients and high-cost services.
- **One to three days before the visit:** Run batch eligibility for all scheduled appointments. This is when most issues are caught.
- **At check-in:** Scan the insurance card, confirm demographics, and recheck if anything has changed.

## How verification is done

**Electronic eligibility (270/271 transactions)** returns coverage information from payers through your practice management system or clearinghouse within seconds. It is efficient for most routine checks.

**Payer portals** often provide more detail, such as remaining deductible amounts or authorization requirements.

**Phone calls** are still sometimes needed for complex benefits, such as specialty services or out-of-network coverage. Document the representative’s name, date, and a reference number for every call.

## What to do when you find a problem

- **Inactive coverage:** Contact the patient before the visit to obtain updated insurance or discuss self-pay options. Uninsured and self-pay patients scheduling non-emergency services are generally entitled to a good faith estimate under the No Surprises Act.
- **Out-of-network:** Inform the patient of their potential costs before the visit.
- **Referral or authorization missing:** Obtain it before the service, or reschedule if necessary.
- **High deductible:** Let the patient know their estimated responsibility and collect what is appropriate at the time of service.

## The financial impact

Verification protects revenue in three ways:

1. **Fewer denials.** Claims go to the right payer, with the right information, for covered services.
2. **Better patient collections.** When patients know their responsibility in advance, they are more likely to pay at the time of service, which is far easier than collecting after a statement.
3. **Better patient experience.** Patients appreciate knowing what to expect, and surprise bills are a major source of complaints.

## Building a reliable workflow

- Assign clear ownership for verification each day
- Use batch eligibility tools to check all appointments automatically
- Create a simple exception queue for issues that need follow-up
- Document every verification in the patient’s account
- Track eligibility-related denials monthly to measure progress

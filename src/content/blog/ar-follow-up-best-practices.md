Accounts receivable (A/R) is the money your practice has earned but not yet collected. Every unpaid claim in your A/R is either moving toward payment or moving toward becoming uncollectible. Effective A/R follow-up makes sure it is the former.

## Why claims sit unpaid

Claims stall for many reasons:

- The payer never received the claim
- The claim is pending and the payer is waiting for information — from you, the patient, or another provider
- The claim was denied and the denial was never worked
- The claim was underpaid and the difference was not noticed
- Coordination of benefits or eligibility issues are unresolved

Without regular follow-up, many of these claims simply age until they reach a filing or appeal deadline.

## Organize A/R by aging buckets

Aging buckets group unpaid balances by how long they have been outstanding, typically:

- **0–30 days:** Recently submitted; most should pay without intervention
- **31–60 days:** Should be paid; follow up on any without a response
- **61–90 days:** At risk; needs active work
- **91–120 days:** High risk; approaching many appeal and filing deadlines
- **120+ days:** Often difficult to collect; requires a targeted cleanup

Track the percentage of your A/R in each bucket. A healthy practice typically keeps the share over 90 days well below 20% of insurance A/R. See our guide to [revenue cycle KPIs](/resources/revenue-cycle-kpis).

## Prioritize the work

Not every unpaid claim deserves the same attention. A practical priority order is:

1. **High-dollar claims** at any age
2. **Claims close to timely filing or appeal deadlines** — once the deadline passes, the money is usually gone. Read about [timely filing limits](/resources/timely-filing-limits).
3. **Claims with no payer response** after 30 days — confirm receipt
4. **Denied claims** that can be corrected or appealed
5. **Underpaid claims**, where payment was less than the contracted rate
6. **Low-dollar claims**, which can often be worked in batches

Work queues in your practice management system should sort claims this way, so staff start each day with the most valuable work.

## How to follow up effectively

### Check status online first

Most payers offer claim status through their portals or electronic claim status transactions (276/277). These are faster than phone calls and resolve many questions.

### Call with a purpose

When a call is needed, be prepared with the patient’s ID, date of service, billed amount, and claim number. Ask specific questions:

- Has the claim been received? On what date?
- What is the current status? If pending, what is needed and from whom?
- If denied, what is the specific reason and how can it be corrected?
- What is the appeal deadline and the correct address or portal?
- When can we expect payment?

### Document every action

Record the date, the representative’s name, the reference number, what was learned, and the next step in the account notes. Good documentation prevents duplicate work and supports appeals.

### Set a next action date

Every claim worked should have a follow-up date. Claims without a next step are the ones that get lost.

## Look for patterns

A/R follow-up is not just about individual claims. If you see the same issue repeatedly — a payer asking for medical records, a missing modifier, an eligibility problem — fix it at the source. Share patterns with front desk staff, coders, and providers. Our article on [common claim denial reasons](/resources/common-claim-denial-reasons) covers the most frequent root causes.

## Cleaning up old A/R

Many practices carry a backlog of old claims, especially after staff turnover or a system change. A structured cleanup project:

1. **Stratify** the old A/R by payer, age, and amount
2. **Identify what is still collectible** based on appeal and filing deadlines
3. **Work the collectible claims** with the highest value first
4. **Appeal** where there is a valid basis
5. **Write off** what is truly uncollectible, with proper adjustment codes and documentation
6. **Analyze** why the claims aged, and change the process

Cleaning up old A/R gives you an accurate picture of what you can really expect to collect.
